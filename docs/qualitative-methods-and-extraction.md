# Qualitative methods and useful interview extraction

Three separate agent reviews checked methodological guidance for analysis, extraction and interface design. This is a methods synthesis, not human researcher validation or a finding about the private interviews. The existing study remains a working Framework analysis.

## What to extract

Start with the research question and the complete source account. Codes index selected passages; interpretation develops through context, comparison, writing and revision.

| Analytical interest | Record in context | Existing workspace support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experiences, practices and processes | What happened, how, who was involved, sequence and consequences | Exact passages, coding and source/case memos |
| Values and emotions | Expressed priorities, meanings and feelings; distinguish the account from analyst inference | Code definitions, analytical memos and chart summaries |
| Conditions and constraints | Place, institutional setting, relationships, resources and circumstances | Source attributes, cases, context and chart qualifications |
| Tensions and differences | Contradictions, qualifications, divergent accounts and alternative explanations | Supporting/contrasting citations and within-/cross-source comparison |
| Interpretive propositions | What the account suggests, its scope and what supports or complicates that reading | Editable, attributed Framework summaries and theme/memo development |
| Uncertainty and missingness | Not asked, not discussed, not coded, unclear wording, restricted or uncharted | Explicit findings/review states and explanatory memos; a blank cell is not an absence finding |
| Researcher influence | Assumptions, perspective, relationships and changes in reasoning | Study setup, private/shared memos and decision history |

Keep exact supporting quotations and source context alongside interpretations. Do not infer participant identity from a filename or treat an institutional claim as independently established fact. An internal inclusion decision is distinct from permission to publish an attributed quotation.

## Choose the method deliberately

- **Framework Method:** familiarization, coding, a working analytical framework, indexing, charting and interpretation support systematic within- and cross-case comparison. Preserve the original account through linked summaries, evidence and analytical memos. Categories can remain topical; explanations require interpretive reasoning.
- **Reflexive thematic analysis:** codes and themes evolve recursively through the researcher's situated interpretation. A theme organizes shared meaning around a central concept. Fixed codebooks, coder consensus and reliability coefficients are not universal requirements of this approach.
- **Qualitative content analysis:** document how categories originate, their definitions/examples, coding rules, abstraction and revisions. Conventional, directed and summative approaches differ; frequency is insufficient by itself to explain meaning or context.

Counts describe the selected coding and its named unit. They cannot establish importance, population prevalence, saturation, participant agreement or human inter-coder reliability. Agent comparison is a simulated review workflow, not independent human coding.

## Implications for the interface

ASR segments, speaker turns, grammatical sentences and analytical meaning units are different. Sentence/segment presentation preserves canonical text, timing and Unicode quotation offsets and permits selections across units. Speaker colors identify supplied labels; neutral Unassigned means speaker separation is absent. Code colors identify analytical categories and use a separate named legend. A distinct display palette can be selected without rewriting the saved codebook; saved colors remain available and native exports preserve saved metadata.

Interpretation prompts should ask what the source describes, in which circumstances, what supports the reading, what complicates it and what remains uncertain. Source verification, speaker attribution, inclusion/consent, agent review and researcher judgment remain separate. Visualizations should retrieve exact contextual evidence rather than calculate automatic findings.

## Sources and verification limits

- [Gale et al. (2013), Framework Method](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3848812/): primary open full text inspected; independently refreshed via the publisher/Europe PMC.
- [Braun and Clarke, Doing reflexive TA](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/doing-reflexive-ta/) and [methodological FAQs](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/): author guidance inspected. Access differed between reviewers; claims follow successful reads, not a claim that every agent accessed every page.
- [Mayring (2000), Qualitative Content Analysis](https://www.qualitative-research.net/index.php/fqs/article/view/1089): primary open full text inspected.
- [Hsieh and Shannon (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16204405/) and [Graneheim and Lundman (2004)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14769454/): author abstracts inspected; no full-text audit claimed.
- [Surrey CAQDAS: analytic tasks and tools](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/computer-assisted-qualitative-data-analysis/resources/analytic-tasks-and-caqdas-tools): institutional guidance inspected in full.
- [COREQ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17872937/) and [SRQR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24979285/): primary publication records and author abstracts inspected. These support transparent reporting, not automatic checklist scoring or an item-by-item compliance claim.

No private interview material was transmitted to literature sources. These reviews guide design and interpretation; they do not replace the chosen methodology, researcher expertise or participant permissions.
