# Qualitative analysis interface: research and design requirements

Research checked on 3 October 2026 (America/Vancouver). This document distinguishes published method guidance from product design recommendations. It does not prescribe a single qualitative methodology or certify research quality.

The interface should help a researcher move between the research question, a complete source account, working codes, analytical interpretation and supporting or contrasting evidence. A chart or completed review task is a record of work; it does not establish accurate transcription, verified participant identity, methodological completeness or human agreement.

## Sources and what they support

### 1. Framework Method: Gale and colleagues, 2013

[Primary open-access article](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-117), also available in [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3848812/).

Gale NK, Heath G, Cameron E, Rashid S, Redwood S. *Using the framework method for the analysis of qualitative data in multi-disciplinary health research*. BMC Medical Research Methodology. 2013;13:117.

The article describes a matrix of cases and categories, retaining connections to individual accounts and enabling comparison within and between cases. Familiarization covers the whole interview, recordings/transcripts and contextual or reflective notes. Charting reduces data while retaining meaning and references to illustrative quotations. Interpretation involves analytical notes, comparisons, relationships, differences and attention to anomalies or empty cells. The authors describe the Framework Method as systematic and flexible and explicitly require qualitative interpretive expertise and reflexivity.

Short source quotation, from the discussion of charting: “balance between reducing the data on the one hand and retaining the original meanings”.

**Design implication:** show concise chart summaries with immediate access to original source context, supporting and contrasting passages, researcher notes and source scope. Do not turn every quotation into a full-width permanent block ahead of the working controls. Preserve the ability to investigate an empty cell rather than label it as absence.

**Method distinction:** the paper's seven stages are transcription; familiarization; coding; developing a working analytical framework; applying the framework; charting; interpretation. Qualityer's current seven workflow stages start after import and add critical review. They are an application workflow informed by the method, not a verbatim reproduction of the paper's seven stages. The method permits inductive, deductive and combined approaches; a new codebook need not be forced into every study.

### 2. Braun and Clarke: reflexive thematic analysis

Primary author guidance: [Doing reflexive TA](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/doing-reflexive-ta/), [methodological FAQs](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/) and [quality guidance](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/quality-in-ta/).

The authors describe a reflexive and recursive process: familiarization; coding; generating initial themes; developing and reviewing themes; refining, defining and naming themes; writing up. These phases support skilled engagement with data rather than a rigid sequence. Themes concern patterns of meaning organized around a central concept. Codes, candidate themes and analytical narrative can change as the researcher returns to the data.

Short source quotation, from the process guidance: “These phases do not prescribe a rigid process you must follow.”

The FAQ distinguishes reflexive TA, coding reliability TA and codebook approaches, explicitly including framework analysis among codebook approaches. Reflexive TA treats meaning as open to situated interpretation and coding as researcher-involved activity; inter-coder reliability is not its universal test of quality. The FAQ also cautions against presenting themes as having simply emerged without analytical work.

**Design implication:** support revisiting stages, evolving notes and themes, and visible researcher authorship. Keep coder comparisons available where appropriate to the chosen approach, but do not require a reliability score, a fixed codebook, independent coders or unanimous acceptance before every study may proceed. Show the method used by the current study; offering a reference to reflexive TA does not mean its workflow has been implemented or that a Framework analysis should be relabeled as reflexive TA.

### 3. Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research: O'Brien and colleagues, 2014

[Primary publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000000388), [author abstract in PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24979285/) and [EQUATOR record](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/srqr/).

O'Brien BC, Harris IB, Beckman TJ, Reed DA, Cook DA. *Standards for reporting qualitative research: a synthesis of recommendations*. Academic Medicine. 2014;89(9):1245–1251.

The author abstract verifies 21 reporting items and an aim of transparency across qualitative research while preserving flexibility across paradigms, approaches and methods. It describes SRQR as a reporting resource for authors, reviewers and readers.

Short source quotation, from the abstract: “preserving the requisite flexibility to accommodate various paradigms, approaches, and methods”.

**Design implication:** provide a readable method-and-provenance summary and retain enough information to explain source preparation, scope, analytical steps and decisions in a report. This is an interface recommendation grounded in the transparency goal and the other full-text method guidance above; this review does not claim to have validated every SRQR item or implemented a complete SRQR reporting checklist. A quality score calculated from filled fields would overstate what reporting completeness establishes.

### 4. COREQ: Tong, Sainsbury and Craig, 2007

[Primary publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/intqhc/mzm042), [EQUATOR record](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/coreq/), and [author abstract indexed by Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17872937).

Tong A, Sainsbury P, Craig J. *Consolidated criteria for reporting qualitative research (COREQ): a 32-item checklist for interviews and focus groups*. International Journal for Quality in Health Care. 2007;19(6):349–357.

The author abstract confirms 32 items for interviews and focus groups, grouped into research team/reflexivity, study design, and data analysis/reporting. It describes sampling, collection setting and method, recording, derivation of themes and supporting quotations among frequently included checklist concerns.

Short source quotation, from the abstract: “description of the derivation of themes and inclusion of supporting quotations”.

**Design implication:** preserve study and interview context, analytical authorship, how themes were developed and traceable quotations. Keep missing or unverified context explicit. COREQ is reporting guidance for a particular study type; it does not authorize manufacturing missing interviewer characteristics, participant identities or member checking. Item-level checklist completion and COREQ compliance were not audited in this interface review.

### 5. CAQDAS Networking Project, University of Surrey

Primary institutional guidance: [Planning for CAQDAS use](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/computer-assisted-qualitative-data-analysis/resources/planning-caqdas-use), [Analytic tasks and CAQDAS tools](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/computer-assisted-qualitative-data-analysis/resources/analytic-tasks-and-caqdas-tools), and [Choosing an appropriate CAQDAS package](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/computer-assisted-qualitative-data-analysis/resources/choosing-appropriate-caqdas-package).

The planning guidance separates research design, methodology and analytic approach from software-project setup. The analytical-tools guidance connects coding, retrieval, linking, writing and visualization. It discusses within-source and cross-source retrieval, maintaining a balance between summarized information and source context, and moving between analytical tasks rather than simply accumulating codes. Coding indexes material; further interpretive work is needed to explain relationships.

Short source quotation, from the analytical-tools guidance: “Coding is essentially a process of cataloguing or indexing data”.

**Design implication:** organize the interface around researcher tasks and movement between source, interpretation and evidence. Show a clear unit and scope for numerical displays, and let a chart open the source material behind it. Frequency and co-occurrence can be useful analytical aids, but the interface should not automatically convert their magnitude into theme importance, population prevalence or causal evidence.

## Requirements mapped to Qualityer

These are design recommendations derived from the sources. A method paper supports the task and analytical principle; it does not prove that a particular layout improves usability. Layout choices should also be checked against the actual project on desktop and a narrow viewport.

| Requirement | Research basis | Interface mapping | Review criterion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep question, method and unit of analysis visible | Gale; Braun/Clarke; Surrey planning | Compact study introduction in Framework workbench and study overview | A researcher can identify what is being compared without opening raw JSON or guessing whether a row is a recording or verified participant. |
| Separate source review from interpretation review | Gale familiarization; COREQ recording/reporting transparency; SRQR transparency | Distinct source wording, speaker identity, inclusion scope, agent review and final researcher decision indicators | Completing AI review never changes audio or identity verification to verified. |
| Make provenance readable | SRQR; COREQ; Gale auditability | Bounded plain-language method summary; structured details behind a disclosure | JSON, hashes and technical history remain accessible without pushing the current analysis task below the initial viewport. |
| Preserve the whole account while selecting evidence | Gale case context; Surrey retrieval; Braun/Clarke full-data engagement | Source navigation, segment/time links and a contextual passage reader | A selected excerpt can be viewed in adjacent source turns; attribution uncertainty stays visible. |
| Distinguish reading coverage from selected coding | Gale familiarization versus indexing; Braun/Clarke coding guidance | Separate source-read and coded-evidence measures | Full familiarization is not described as exhaustive coding, saturation or completed transcription review. |
| Give chart summaries access to evidence and qualification | Gale charting/interpretation; COREQ quotes; Surrey source context | Source/theme selection with concise summary and supporting/contrasting evidence panels | Summary, limits and both evidence relations can be inspected without scrolling through every full quotation first. |
| Make interpretations editable and attributable | Gale analytical memos/reflexivity; Braun/Clarke situated interpretation | Memo editor, save/review controls, authorship and decision history | The researcher can revise a theme or chart cell; the interpretation stays distinct from immutable source text. |
| Permit revisiting analytical steps | Braun/Clarke recursive process; Gale notes at any stage; Surrey movement between tasks | Stage navigation that remains available across the workflow | A completed stage does not lock earlier notes, code definitions or theme refinement. |
| Preserve meaningful nonfindings | Gale anomalies/empty cells; explicit project scope | Observed, not observed, withheld and not charted states | A blank or restricted cell is never silently converted into a substantive absence claim. |
| Keep disagreement and counterexamples useful | Gale comparison; Braun/Clarke situated interpretation | Contrasting evidence and differences between accounts alongside summaries | The interface supports recording differences without requiring agreement or erasing them through a majority decision. |
| Make count definitions and source filters visible | Surrey summarized information/source context; method-specific interpretation | Analysis workbench measure labels, active filters and drill-through | Applications, distinct excerpts, sources and coverage use distinct labels; counts do not implicitly rank substantive importance. |
| Provide human control over suggestions | Gale experienced interpretive judgment; Braun/Clarke researcher involvement | Pending recommendations, contextual evidence and explicit accept/revise/reject/defer actions | Suggestions produce no applied coding until a permitted decision is made; final AI acceptance is clearly identified as AI review. |
| Keep exports useful for reporting and reinspection | SRQR; COREQ; Gale auditability | Export method, question, unit, scope, summaries, limits, exact citations, decisions and source provenance | A report can explain how an interpretation was reached; transcript-only exports do not imply source audio was reviewed. |

## Actual-project observations informing layout

Read-only inspection of the private two-recording project found a concrete obstacle: structured `methodNotes` were rendered as one large JSON paragraph before the workflow stages and matrix. The current working task was consequently below the first desktop viewport. Opening a chart cell placed its editor beneath the matrix and displayed many long passages before save/review controls. These observations support progressive disclosure, a focused source/theme selection and a bounded contextual evidence pane. They do not justify hiding important limitations or discarding provenance.

A proposed arrangement is a compact study introduction; source and theme selectors; a focused summary/limitations editor with nearby save/review controls; and adjacent evidence tabs or lists that open complete context on selection. Keep all sources/themes reachable, including on a narrow viewport. A table may remain useful as an optional cross-case overview or export; it need not be the only route to working on a cell.

## Implemented interface changes

The study overview now opens by default and presents the question, method, source-specific reading and selected coding, analytical scope and separate agent/researcher review states. A dedicated study-setup screen records the question, approach, rationale, sampling/context, researcher perspective and reporting boundaries, with earlier decisions retained. It changes guidance without relabeling an existing Framework study or imposing a reliability gate on reflexive thematic analysis.

Navigation groups the available screens into Study, Prepare, Code and reflect, Interpret and review, and Explore and report. Team, service and example tools remain accessible in a secondary disclosure. A new study opens its overview; source-card actions open the selected recording. View changes reset page position instead of carrying unrelated scroll positions into the next task.

The Framework workbench offers a compact source-by-theme comparison and a focused interpretation view. Summary, qualifications and save/review actions appear beside a bounded evidence list and surrounding dialogue context. Method provenance is readable behind a disclosure. Supporting and contrasting evidence remain distinct. Reviewing is disabled while the interpretation has unsaved changes, so a visible edited draft cannot accidentally approve an older saved interpretation.

The reading workspace keeps source selection, search, recording access and exact passage navigation visible. Speaker management, coding-range inspection and transcript/timing export sit in a Source tools disclosure. Creating a code from a selected passage keeps the quotation selected but requires a separate Apply coding action. Source review can preview that exact selection, showing surrounding text separately; an off-record phrase offers a candidate scope preview without applying a decision. Scope changes still require explicit reasoning and the existing permission checks.

Processing starts with completed tasks visible when no tasks remain pending. Its status text separates agent-reviewed chart cells from recorded researcher review. Code-reading labels now consistently refer to codes rather than presenting each code as an analytical theme. These changes improve task orientation; they do not replace qualitative expertise or establish usability with actual researchers.

## Scope of the private-recording analysis

The private audit confirmed that both complete supplied machine drafts were familiarized: 2,065 segments in total. The first recording had 905 segments read, 46 exact selected passage ranges, 86 code applications and six charted themes. The second had 1,160 segments, 48 selected ranges and 96 applications. This is 94 selected passage ranges and 182 multi-code applications, not 182 independent observations. The application preserved the original draft text, supplied timing and Unassigned speaker labels; it did not substitute a later live transcription.

The independent AI critic accepted 92 passage interpretations and requested two revisions, which were applied. That exercise verifies a simulated recommendation/review workflow and exact source anchoring. Audio wording, identities, institutional assertions, participant publication consent and real researcher final decisions remain unverified. No human inter-coder reliability or saturation claim follows from these counts. Detailed source material and private audit artifacts remain outside this repository.

## Source-access limits

The Gale article, Braun/Clarke author guidance and Surrey guidance were read in full through the links above. SRQR's author abstract and EQUATOR listing were inspected. COREQ's author abstract was inspected through Europe PMC, together with its EQUATOR listing. The SRQR and COREQ publisher full-text pages returned HTTP 403 in this environment; this document therefore limits claims about them to verified abstract/listing content rather than asserting an item-by-item full-text audit. Reporting guidelines should be applied with the chosen methodology and study context, not turned into compulsory software scoring rules.
